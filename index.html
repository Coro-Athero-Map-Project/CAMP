Coro-Athero-Map Project
Coro-Athero-Map Project (CAMP) is a prospective longitudinal cohort and biobank study designed to systematically delineate the molecular landscape of coronary atherosclerosis by integrating deep multi-omics profiling of coronary plaque tissues, blood molecular signatures, imaging, and long-term clinical follow-up.
Overall objective
To establish a research platform integrating a coronary tissue biobank, blood biobank, multi-omics data, deep clinical phenotyping, and long-term follow-up, and through an open-access coronary reference atlas and AI-powered analytical tools, to drive the transition of CAD from conventional empirical therapy to molecular subtype-guided precision medicine.

Research Overview

To date, 309 patients have been enrolled in the CE cohort. The HTx cohort offers a unique window into normal or early-stage coronary specimens, covering the continuous spectrum of lesions from early intimal thickening to advanced complex plaques. The team previously established the first single-cell transcriptomic atlas of normal human cardiac arteries, performed proteomic profiling on 20 coronary specimens spanning five histopathological stages, and laid a solid technical and data foundation for the construction of the CAMP coronary reference atlas.

Leveraging two unique clinical scenarios, we are integrating artificial intelligence-based modeling with multi-omics data to construct an open-access coronary reference atlas and a digital twin platform, aiming to drive the transition of CAD from conventional empirical therapy to molecular subtype-guided precision medicine.
